Services

Benhaven answers fire, emergency medical, and rescue calls, and it has long described prevention work in the community.

Fire

Benhaven provides fire response for the community it serves. Crews work to protect people first, then property and the environment. The department does not publish response-time promises on this website.

EMS

Emergency medical service has been part of Benhaven since it was organized for both fire protection and ambulance and rescue service. Paid members, volunteers, and dual-certified members may answer a medical call. Clinical levels and transport destinations are not listed here because a current clinical roster has not been published.

Rescue

The department’s public name includes rescue. A 2014 public post described rescue apparatus. Specific rescue capabilities will be described when the department publishes an equipment list. Until then, call 911 and let the dispatcher send what the call needs.

Mutual aid

When a call needs more people or equipment than one department brings, fire and EMS agencies assist one another. Benhaven takes part in that mutual-aid system. Neighboring departments are not named on this site until Benhaven confirms the list it wants published.

Prevention and inspections

The department’s earlier website described work beyond emergency response:

  • Community outreach. Crews have taken apparatus to schools and day-care facilities to teach fire prevention, and to let children see and hear firefighters so they are less likely to hide during a fire.
  • Public questions. The department has offered prevention and safety information to neighbors and invited questions.
  • Event staffing. The department has described providing fire and EMS staff at community events, including Western Harnett athletic events, so an injury on site can get prompt care.
  • Commercial inspections. The department has described inspections of commercial buildings in its area to check that safety requirements are being met. Fees, cycles, and the current inspection process are not published here. Call 919-499-9511 to ask.
  • Burn permits. Outdoor burning has its own page, including the state rules and the local phone number. Read the burn permit guide.
  • Junior firefighters. The department has welcomed youth who are at least 16 and interested in a firefighting career. Details are on the Join Us page.

What to expect when you call 911

This is general guidance for our community. The 911 center’s own procedures control the call.

  1. Dial 911 for a fire, a medical emergency, a rescue, or any situation that needs immediate help. The station phone is not the emergency line.
  2. Tell the dispatcher where you are. A street address is best. On a highway, give the road, the direction of travel, and the nearest crossroad or marker you know.
  3. Say what is happening and who needs help. Answer the questions you are asked. Those questions choose the right crews while help is already being started.
  4. Stay on the line until the dispatcher says you can hang up.
  5. If it is safe, unlock a door, turn on an outside light, and keep the driveway clear. Keep people and pets back when apparatus arrives.
  6. You may see a fire engine, an ambulance, or both. Benhaven is a fire and EMS department, and crews often work the same call together.
  7. Follow the crew’s instructions. Do not follow an emergency vehicle closely.

For a question that can wait, call 919-499-9511.
