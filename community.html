Community education

Practical fire-safety habits for home, plus the programs the department has publicly described.

Home fire safety

These are standard home-safety habits. They are not a report of local fire statistics.

  • Install smoke alarms on every level of the home, inside bedrooms, and outside sleeping areas. Test them monthly. Replace an alarm that is more than 10 years old, or when the manufacturer says to.
  • Make an escape plan with two ways out of each room and a meeting place outside. Practice it.
  • Stay in the kitchen while you cook. Keep a lid nearby for a pan fire. If grease catches fire, cover the pan and turn off the heat. Do not throw water on a grease fire.
  • Keep space heaters at least 3 feet from anything that can burn, and plug them directly into a wall outlet.
  • Smoke outside, use a sturdy ashtray, and soak ashes in water before you throw them away.
  • Store matches and lighters up high, out of the reach and sight of children.
  • Close doors at night. A closed door can slow smoke and fire.

Through the year

  • Spring. Check alarms when you change the clocks. Charge phones before storms, and know how you would leave if a tree blocks the drive.
  • Summer. Grill outdoors, away from siding, eaves, and overhanging branches. Never grill on an enclosed porch. Keep a hose or extinguisher nearby.
  • Fall. October is Fire Prevention Month. Before heating season, have chimneys and furnaces checked by a qualified technician. The department has used this month for school visits. Read the note on those visits.
  • Winter. Keep portable heaters in the open, and blow out candles before you leave a room or go to sleep. Do not use an oven to heat a home.

In the schools

The department has publicly described taking fire and EMS apparatus to schools and day-care facilities. Crews dress in turnout gear and breathing apparatus so children can see and hear a firefighter, and so they learn not to hide. A public account of one visit names Benhaven Elementary School. Class times for a future visit are not posted. Schools can call 919-499-9511 to ask.

Hydrants

When crews flow hydrants, tap water can look rusty for a short time. Run cold water until it clears before you wash clothes. Crews work at the edge of the road in reflective clothing. Slow down. The full note is on the hydrant testing page.

Burn safety

Outdoor burning in North Carolina is limited. A permit does not make an illegal fire legal, and a burn ban overrides a permit. Read the burn permit guide before you light a pile, and call 919-499-9511.
