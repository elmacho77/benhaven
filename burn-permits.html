Burn permits

A plain-language guide for neighbors. It is not a permit, and it is not legal advice.

If this page and a state rule ever disagree, follow the state rule and call the department before you burn. Official pages are linked below. A burn ban and a permit are different things. A permit does not override a ban.

Burn-ban banner. When the department posts a burn ban, a dark grey banner appears at the top of every page, including this one. The banner is off unless it has been turned on. No banner does not mean burning is allowed. Also check the N.C. Forest Service, and call 919-499-9511.

Request a burn permit

Burn permits for this area are issued online by the N.C. Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. A permit does not excuse anyone from the air-quality rules below, and it does not override a burn ban.

Open the burn permit application (opens in a new tab)

  1. Open the application.
  2. In the county dropdown, select Harnett.
  3. Complete the rest of the form.

If you need help with the form, call 919-499-9511. County ranger contacts are listed by the N.C. Forest Service (opens in a new tab). Their permit FAQ has listed a burn-permit support line at 919-578-2207.

What North Carolina allows

The N.C. Division of Air Quality prohibits most open burning. Some fires are allowed without an air-quality permit only when every condition of the rule is met. Read the rule here: N.C. DEQ open burning (opens in a new tab).

Do not burn

  • Trash or garbage
  • Paper, newspaper, or cardboard
  • Plastic, tires, or other rubber
  • Metal, including wire
  • Treated, painted, or coated wood
  • Lumber and other building materials, asphalt shingles, heavy oils, paints, or household and agricultural chemicals
  • Buildings, including outbuildings and mobile homes
  • Anything man-made, artificial, or synthetic

What may be burned, if the conditions are met

  • Leaves, limbs, brush, firewood, and other natural vegetation from your own yard
  • Logs, stumps, and other plant growth from land clearing, which has extra distance rules

For yard vegetation, the published exception generally requires all of the following. This is a summary, not a substitute for the rule:

  • The material comes from that private residence and is burned on the same property. Do not combine debris from several homes.
  • There is no public pickup service for that material.
  • No trash or other non-vegetative material is in the pile.
  • The fire is started no earlier than 8:00 a.m., and no material is added between 6:00 p.m. and 8:00 a.m. the next day.
  • The fire does not create a nuisance.
  • No burn ban is in effect.
  • The burn is not started on a day forecast as Code Orange or higher for that county. Check the North Carolina air-quality forecast (opens in a new tab).

Land-clearing piles have further limits. Published guidance says they must be at least 500 feet from occupied structures, and at least 250 feet from a public road when the wind blows toward the road. Smoke should be carried away from roads and places where people are. Kerosene or diesel may be used to start a land-clearing fire. No other man-made material may be burned. A regional air-quality supervisor, not this website, can consider a written request to burn closer than 500 feet. Do not use gasoline to start any fire.

Campfires, outdoor cooking fires, and similar small fires are treated separately in the state rules. When you are unsure which kind of fire you have, do not light it. Call the non-emergency line.

Safety checklist

  • Read the state rules and confirm your fire is an allowed type.
  • Check the air-quality forecast. Do not start a burn on Code Orange or higher.
  • Check for a Forest Service or local burn ban, and look for a dark grey banner on this website.
  • Request a permit at the state application and select Harnett in the county dropdown.
  • Call Benhaven at 919-499-9511 before you burn.
  • Keep the pile small. Stay with it. Keep water or a hose within reach.
  • Keep clearance from homes, woods, fences, vehicles, and roads.
  • Do not burn trash, and do not leave the fire.
  • Extinguish the fire until it is cold, with no smoke and no heat.
  • If the fire escapes or you cannot control it, get to a safe place and call 911.

Summary prepared November 5, 2026. Rules change. Confirm them the day you plan to burn.
